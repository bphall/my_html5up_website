Brayton Hall

Data Scientist · Language Models · Philosophy and Literature

For the past several years I have worked on language models at large banks, mostly on the problem of usefully leveraging those models in a safe and scalable way. Models from Anthropic and OpenAI are becoming superhuman, but I still believe there's an art at play that goes beyond prompt engineering.

The deep math, the mechanisms of transformers and reinforcement learning, and the recursive loop between human behavior and AI acceleration are a critical new region of interest somewhere between math and philosophy and computer science, which is where I've lived for years. I left Capital One and moved back to NYC in August 2026: check back soon for some exciting updates...

Before this

I studied philosophy, metalogic and number theory at UNC Chapel Hill, before teaching logic and statistics at Virginia Tech. My research was on the history of mathematical logic and the philosophy of language, particularly in the early 20th century, with a focus on the philosophy of Ludwig Wittgenstein. The subject of my thesis was on the scientific explanation of events in history, and ranged over canonical twentieth century texts including Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations and Thomas Kuhn's The Structure of Scientific Revolutions.

Off the clock: long runs (the occasional marathon), New York by ferry, movies at the Metrograph, the piano, writing, and slow, happy progress through Joyce and Proust.
