Data Scientist · Language Models · Philosophy and Literature

Stitched from Ulysses. Hover a word: its neighbors answer, by cosine similarity between word vectors learned from Joyce’s own prose (PPMI and SVD over co-occurrence, the classic recipe). Words also summon the parts of the photo they name, and patches of the photo summon words: five anchors (sea, sky, stone, grass, foam) bridge the two spaces. Dotted words are coinages: they appear once in the language, so no statistics can hold them.

Both panes are the same one-line idea:
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKT / √dk) V
The thing under your cursor is the query, everything else is a key, the normalization plays softmax, and the lighting is the value weighting. Make Q, K, V learned instead of hand-built, scale to thousands of dimensions, stack it deep, and you have a transformer. For the real thing built from scratch, watch Andrej Karpathy’s Zero to Hero: the best classroom on the internet.

I build with large language models: summarization, classification, and extraction over huge volumes of sensitive text, and the harnesses that turn a raw model into a system you can trust. Lately that means agents, evals, and workflows. I follow the research closely, from post-training to interpretability, and the question I care about most is what comes next.

The through line, from a philosophy department to production: what it takes for symbols to mean something.

Off the clock: long runs (the occasional marathon), New York by ferry, movies at the Metrograph, the piano, writing, and slow, happy progress through Joyce and Proust.
