Brayton Hall

Data scientist. Language models in production, philosophy and literature always.

portrait

I build systems that read, decide, and explain themselves. Most of that work lives in the unglamorous middle of a pipeline: schemas that hold, evaluations that catch a regression before a customer does, retrieval that returns the paragraph a person actually needed rather than the one that scored well.

What holds my attention now is the deep math, the mechanisms inside transformers and reinforcement learning, and the recursive loop between human behaviour and machine acceleration. It sits somewhere between mathematics, philosophy and computer science, which is roughly where I have lived for years.

Voice intake for a clinical practice

A telephone agent that takes a patient call end to end, writes the note, and hands a clean structured record to the practice management system. The hard part was never the model. It was the handoff.

Ranking pipelines with an honest offline harness. If an evaluation cannot embarrass you, it is decoration.

2020 to 2022

Forecasting for operations

Demand models that people on a floor actually used, which meant explaining every number twice and removing half of them.

A model is a sentence about the world, and like any sentence it is only as good as what it leaves out.

Notes

Writing

Short pieces, mostly about the seam where statistics stops being a calculation and starts being an argument. I write them slowly and revise them in public, which is a polite way of saying the older ones are wrong in places I have not found yet.

The recurring subject is memory: what a system keeps, what it discards, and who decided. That question turns out to be the same question in a database, in a novel, and in a person, which is why the three keep ending up in the same paragraph.
